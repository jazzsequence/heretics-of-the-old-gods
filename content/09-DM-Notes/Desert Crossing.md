
# Desert Crossing

Rules for foot travel across the open flats of the [[Hellcatch Valley]] — and
Nuxan's deep desert generally — where purple worms hunt by ground-rhythm.
Crawlers bypass all of this (see [[Rig Rules]]). Self-contained.

## The Threat

Purple worms (standard stat block, CR 15) sense vibration through tremorsense
and are drawn from a distance to the steady cadence of a natural walking gait or
a rolling wheel. On the open flats, a party on foot walking normally draws one to
itself with every step. A CR 15 worm against a low-level party is not a fight —
it's a disaster to survive.

Three things keep people alive:

- **Cities.** Constant industrial noise floods the ground and worms won't
  approach it. Settlements sit on effectively safe ground; walking in and
  immediately around a desert city is fine.
- **Rigs.** Crawlers run subsonic gear that scrambles worm tremorsense. Rig
  travel across the flats is safe barring gear failure — which is why reaching or
  owning a rig is the real key to leaving on your own terms.
- **[[The Weathered]].** Deshretu's survivors (see [[Deshretu]]) walk the flats in
  a trained, broken-cadence gait that never settles into a rhythm a worm can read.
  Earned knowledge, taught not bought — an outsider doesn't have it.

## The Gate

A party with no rig, no technique, and no Weathered guide **cannot cross the open
flats on foot.** They don't get a check — natural walking draws a worm, full
stop. Leaving Bassuras overland requires acquiring one of the three: the
technique is a relationship (the Weathered), the rig is debt or ownership. Until
then the desert is the wall.

## Crossing on Foot

Once the party has the technique or a guide, foot crossing is possible but stays
dangerous. Resolve as repeated group checks, never one roll.

**Cadence.** One **group Wisdom (Survival) check per leg** — a leg being roughly
a half-day of open-flats travel (set granularity to taste: per watch, per terrain
change, per half-day). A full crossing is several legs. The repetition is the
point; the crawler is meant to look better with every check.

**The check.** Everyone rolls Survival. Apply the group-success rule *and* count
individual failures:

- **Half or more succeed → the leg passes.** If anyone failed, it's a
  **near-miss**: distant worm-sign, ground lurching underfoot, a spent resource
  (water, a spooked mount), lost time. No attack — the steady walkers covered the
  broken gaits. Narrate that the thing is out there.
- **Fewer than half succeed → a worm is drawn.** It surfaces on the party. Run it
  as a catastrophe to escape, not a winnable fight (below).

Baseline **DC 12** — clear day, open flats, unladen, technique known.

## Modifiers

Two buckets, kept separate on purpose. Conditions move the **DC**; walker state
grants **advantage/disadvantage**. (2024 disadvantage doesn't stack — pile every
factor onto adv/disadv and most are wasted. Route conditions through the DC so
they register.)

**DC adjustments — the crossing:**

| Condition | DC |
|---|---|
| Baseline — clear, open flats, daylight, technique known | 12 |
| Sandstorm or high wind | +3 |
| Deep flats, far from any city-noise | +2 |
| Worm-active season / recent tremors | +2 |
| Night (worms nearer the surface) | +2 |
| Nearing city-noise, ruins, or bedrock (dead ground) | −2 to −5 |

**Advantage / disadvantage — the walkers:**

- **Weathered guide setting the pace:** advantage. Survivable, never safe — the
  guide still rolls, the party can still fail.
- **Heavy armor:** disadvantage.
- **Encumbered, or hauling noisy/heavy cargo:** disadvantage.
- **Forced march / moving fast:** disadvantage.
- These don't compound — any one imposes disadvantage, the rest are flavor. Which
  is why the real choice is *leave light or don't leave*.

**Exhaustion** rides on top via the normal rules (forced march, heat, no water) —
−2 per level to all d20 tests in 2024. An exhausted armored walker eats
disadvantage *and* a stacking flat penalty on the same check, and degrades each
leg; armor and encumbrance accrue it faster. Night crossings cut the heat
pressure but raise the worm risk — a real tradeoff, not a strictly worse option.
Don't fold exhaustion into disadvantage; let it be its own penalty.

## When a Worm Is Drawn

A CR 15 worm kills a low-level party in a straight fight, so don't run one. It
surfaces at distance and telegraphs; the party gets a beat. Escape means breaking
the rhythm it locked onto — go still, reach dead ground (bedrock, ruins,
city-noise), or reach a rig and its deterrent. A worm that misses is slow to
re-acquire. Losing the mounts, the cargo, or a rig to buy the escape is the
intended price. A TPK should come from compounding bad calls, not one failed
group roll.

## Texture

Occasionally surface a worm on a *clean* leg — breaching on the horizon, pacing
the group for a mile, taking a lone pack animal and going back down. It's a
hunting animal, not a skill tax. Once or twice a campaign, not per crossing, or
it stops meaning anything.

## Quick Reference

- No rig, no technique, no guide → can't cross on foot. Worm, no roll.
- Otherwise: group Survival per leg, DC 12 ± conditions, adv/disadv from state.
- Half+ succeed = pass (near-miss if anyone failed). Under half = worm surfaces.
- Worm = escape scenario, not a fight.
